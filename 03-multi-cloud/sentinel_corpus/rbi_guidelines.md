# RBI Guidelines on Digital Payment Fraud Prevention

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) mandates that all licensed Payment System Operators (PSOs) and authorized banking entities implement robust fraud-prevention frameworks for digital payment systems including Unified Payments Interface (UPI), National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT), and Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS). Financial institutions must deploy real-time transaction monitoring systems that flag anomalous patterns such as unusually high-value transfers, rapid successive transactions from a single account, and transactions to known high-risk beneficiary accounts. The guidelines require that every digital payment transaction be authenticated through at least two independent factors (2FA) and that merchants obtain explicit customer consent for each transaction.

Under the RBI's Master Direction on Fraud Monitoring and Control (2024), banks and PSOs must report all confirmed fraud incidents to the Centralised Fraud Registry (CFR) within two working days of detection. Institutions are required to maintain a Fraud Prevention, Detection, and Control Framework (FPDCF) that includes periodic risk assessments, independent internal audits, and customer education programmes. The RBI further directs that all entities operating digital payment infrastructure must implement machine-learning-based anomaly detection capable of analysing behavioural biometrics, device fingerprinting, and geolocation data to identify synthetic identities and account takeover attempts in real time.

For UPI-specific fraud prevention, the RBI requires that Payment Service Providers (PSPs) implement velocity checks, daily transaction limits, and mandatory cooling-off periods for adding new Virtual Payment Addresses (VPAs) or beneficiary accounts. Any UPI transaction exceeding ₹2 lakh must trigger an additional authentication step beyond the standard UPI PIN. The RBI also mandates that PSPs maintain transaction logs for a minimum of five years and make them available for regulatory audits. Non-compliance with these guidelines can result in penalties, suspension of payment processing privileges, or revocation of operating licences.

Financial institutions operating under these guidelines must also establish a dedicated Grievance Redressal cell that acknowledges fraud complaints within four hours and provides provisional credit to affected customers within seven working days pending investigation. The RBI's framework further requires that all fraud cases involving amounts exceeding ₹50,000 be escalated to the institution's Chief Risk Officer and reported to the banking ombudsman. These measures collectively aim to create a resilient digital payments ecosystem while protecting consumers from evolving fraud threats in India's rapidly growing digital economy.
