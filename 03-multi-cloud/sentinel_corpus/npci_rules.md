# NPCI Rules for Transaction Monitoring

The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) governs the operational parameters of the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) system, which processes over 10 billion transactions monthly. Under NPCI's operational guidelines, each UPI transaction must be subject to automated monitoring rules that verify the authenticity of the transaction initiator, the legitimacy of the receiving account, and the consistency of the transaction pattern with the account holder's historical behaviour. NPCI mandates that all participating banks and Third-Party Application Providers (TPAPs) implement end-to-end encryption for all UPI messages and enforce mutual TLS (mTLS) authentication between all nodes in the payment switch infrastructure.

NPCI enforces strict transaction limits to manage fraud exposure: individual UPI transactions are capped at ₹5 lakh per day for inter-bank transfers and ₹1 lakh per day for intra-bank transfers. For merchant payments, the daily limit is ₹5 lakh per customer per merchant. Velocity monitoring rules require that any account initiating more than 10 transactions within a 15-minute window be flagged for manual review, and accounts exceeding 50 transactions in a single hour are automatically suspended pending identity verification. These thresholds are configurable per-bank but must not exceed the NPCI-mandated maximums. All exceptions must be logged and reported to NPCI's monitoring dashboard within the same business day.

The NPCI's Fraud Monitoring Guidelines (Version 4.2, 2024) require all member banks to implement a real-time Transaction Risk Engine (TRE) that scores each UPI transaction on a scale of 0–1000 based on factors including the device used, the time of transaction, the geographic location, the transaction amount relative to the account's average, and the reputation score of the beneficiary account. Transactions scoring above 700 must be held in a quarantine queue for additional authentication steps, while those above 900 must be rejected outright and reported to the NPCI Fraud Intelligence Unit. Banks must maintain a false-positive rate below 0.5% to avoid excessive customer friction.

NPCI also mandates quarterly reporting on fraud metrics including total fraud value prevented, mean time to detect (MTTD), mean time to respond (MTTR), and customer recovery rates. All member institutions must participate in NPCI's shared intelligence network, where anonymised fraud indicators are exchanged in real time across the UPI ecosystem. This collective defence model enables early detection of coordinated fraud rings and emerging attack vectors. Banks that consistently fail to meet NPCI's fraud monitoring standards risk downgrading in the compliance matrix, which can affect their participation privileges in new UPI product rollouts.
